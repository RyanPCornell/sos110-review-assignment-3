Before You Build

Required for Module 6. As you watch, pay attention to the river’s three components:

  1. Social systemswho the river serves, who lives beside it, and who decides
  2. Ecological (natural) systemsthe water, habitat and hazards the river carries
  3. Technological systemsthe concrete, the engineering, and what it was built to do

Then weigh the trade-offs and feedback loops in both stories: building the concrete channel, and the proposal to restore the river.

Create a digital poster that shows the systems dynamics of the Los Angeles River Restoration Project as discussed in the video, and answer these questions directly on it.

Socio-economic systems

1What are the key socio-economic benefits of the LA River Restoration? Include at least one example from the video.

2Were there any social equity or justice issues in the past or anticipated in the future restoration work? Include at least one specific example.

Ecological systems

1Applying the concept of ecological resilience, what are the key benefits of the LA River Restoration?

2What are some potential ecosystem challenges (e.g., changes in hazards or risks, ecosystem services, biogeochemical cycles)?

Technical systems

1What are the key technical or technological components historically associated with the LA River system?

2How did the engineering solutions for LA River management impact the ecosystems and communities?

How this works

The builder makes the poster the assignment asks for: a title band, a diagram of the river as three linked systems, text boxes answering the six questions, and a summary. Save as PDF lays all of it out on one landscape sheet.

The symbols
System dynamics, on the canvas

Boundaries. The dashed frame is the system. A parallelogram shows what enters or leaves it. Interconnectedness. Each lane is one system; an arrow from one lane to another is a link between systems. Feedback loops. Label arrows + (the two move the same way) or − (opposite ways). When arrows close a circle, the panel names the loop: reinforcing if it has an even number of − arrows, balancing if odd. Click a loop to light it up. Emergence. Use the emergence callout for a property of the whole system that no single part has.

Trade-offs

Tag each piece to the engineered era (the concrete channel) or the restoration, or leave it for both. The Show switch above the canvas then shows either era alone, so you can see what the channel gave the city and what it took away.

Drawing and saving

Drag a symbol into a lane, or click it. Double-click any piece to rename it, change its symbol or tag its era. → Arrow, + Arrow and − Arrow: drag from one piece to another; clicking an arrow cycles it through the three. Work saves in this browser as you go; Save downloads a file you can Open on any computer.

The worked example is the Salt River in Phoenix: the same kind of story, on a different river, so it shows the shape of a poster without giving away the LA River.
